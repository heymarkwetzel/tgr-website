About Us

Together, we are reclaiming the social structure of care.

The nonprofit sector has been hollowed out by a "business-first" culture that prioritizes extractive data over human relationship. We believe generosity is a durable spectrum of reciprocity, not a series of isolated transactions.

By building social infrastructure grounded in trust, we move beyond immediate firefighting toward a future of sustainable, relational community.

We want to work with you to spearhead this philosophical shift in how civil society organizes care.

The Game

The Generosity Roundtable is a tabletop roleplaying experience for nonprofit leaders, boards, and the communities they serve.

Structured game cards, character roles, and real-world scenarios help groups navigate tradeoffs and make better decisions together.

Each session turns complex challenges into playable conversations. Participants step into roles, respond to prompts, and test decisions before they affect real people and communities.

step 1

Create Character

Build a perspective before the conversation begins.

Participants use Character Creator cards and guided prompts to develop a role rooted in real motivations, responsibilities, and constraints.

Each character includes:

  • Core archetype
  • "No compromise" boundary
  • Key motivations and pressures

This step helps groups move beyond titles and understand how different roles see the same problem.

step 2

Play

Work through a real scenario using structured rounds.

Game cards guide discussion, introduce constraints, and push decisions forward. Scenarios reflect common nonprofit challenges across leadership, boards, and community impact.

Gameplay includes:

  • Scenario prompts
  • Dialogue cards
  • Decision rounds

The format keeps conversations focused and moves groups toward clear outcomes.

step 3

Peer Learning

Reflect, align, and move forward together.

After gameplay, the group reviews what happened, what tensions surfaced, and what decisions were made.

Participants leave with

  • Shared understanding across roles
  • Clearer language for complex challenges
  • Practical next steps

Learning comes from the group itself, grounded in lived experience and collective insight.
